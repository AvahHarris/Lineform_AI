# Update notes – 2026-11-16

## Joint range: 0–180°, 90° = straight

The motors physically move only ±90° off the straight pose. The straight pose itself is unchanged (it does not move physically).

| | Displayed (bars, readouts) | Internal (code, AI, shape library, serial protocol) |
|---|---|---|
| straight | **90** | 180 |
| limits | **0 … 180** | 90 … 270 |

- The internal convention is kept on purpose: the AI solver, animations, agents, and the shape library all use it. Conversion: `toDisplayDeg(x) = x − 90`; limits `JOINT_MIN_DEG` / `JOINT_MAX_DEG` / `clampJoint()` in `Gui_3_0.pde`.
- **Commands are clamped to exactly 0–180** everywhere: bar drags, Create Shape, AI shapes and the solver, animations and waves, holding a hand-moved pose, and leaving manipulation mode. The firmware clamps again (`JOINT_MIN_DEG/MAX_DEG` in `lineform-ai.ino`).
- AI: `AI_MAX_TURN_DEG` 120 → 90. The saturation check uses the new limits. The agents are told each joint can only reach ±90.

## Angle detection is not distorted

- Readings are **never clamped**. The bar itself can only show 0–180, so each row also keeps its true value (`motorShownDeg`).
  - The bar readout is drawn by the GUI (ControlP5's own label would show the internal value). It shows the true angle, e.g. `-12.5°  LIMIT` in red, when a joint was pushed past a limit by hand.
  - The 3D view draws the true angle too. Its old 60–300 clamp is now 0–360.
- Hold logic and overload checks use the raw readings, as before.
- **Leaving manipulation mode / hold-after-push past a limit:** the motor holds AT the limit (it moves back slowly), and the chat lists which motors were moved back. The firmware's `holdHere()` does the same, including on multi-turn positions.
- **Command rounding fixed:** the GUI encoded angles with `int()` and the firmware decoded them with integer `map()`. Both rounded down, so commands were up to 1.44° low. Now both use rounding and float math.

## Tested (host harness around the real firmware functions)

- Commands for internal 0/90/180/270/300/360 → motor goals clamped to 90–270, correct for plain, reversed, and offset (+29°) motors.
- Readings outside the range come back unclamped (e.g. raw 500 → 44°).
- `holdHere()`: in range → exact present position. Past a limit → the limit, in the same turn (e.g. raw 7396 → 7167).

## Note: shape library

Some presets' **"large"** variants use angles beyond the new range: square, zigzag, circle, hook, tennis racket, flat iron, triangle, hexagon, equilateral triangle. They are clamped when applied, so those shapes come out less bent at large sizes. The "small" variants are all within range. Re-save these presets if they matter.

## Later the same day (2): real motor geometry in the 3D view, twitching motors 6/7

### 3D view: real motor geometry, no more overlapping blocks

**Cause.** Every block was a 36 × 36 box. The joint pivot sat on a box *face* (even joints) only ~10 units from the previous block's end, with a 3.8-unit gap. Neighbouring blocks cut into each other from **~13°** of bend.

**Now** the blocks model the real hardware: **XL330-M077-T, 34 × 26 × 20 mm**, joined by bridge brackets with a **0.8 cm gap**. Constants are in `Gui_3_0.pde` → "3D chain geometry":

| constant | value | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `MOTOR_LENGTH_MM` | 34 | along the chain (still drawn 52 units long; 1 mm = 1.53 units) |
| `MOTOR_THICK_MM` / `MOTOR_THIN_MM` | 26 / 20 | the block is thick in the bending plane of the joint at its far end, thin along that joint's axis, so consecutive blocks are turned 90° like the real arm |
| `MOTOR_SHAFT_FROM_END_MM` | 10.5 | pivot = shaft, centred in the body, this far from its end. **Estimate:** measure and adjust if the hinge markers sit off the real shafts |
| `MOTOR_GAP_MM` | 8 | gap between bodies |

- Blocks are centred on the hinge axis (the old half-height sideways offset is gone).
- Dark bridge brackets are drawn across each gap; they turn with the joint.
- Checked numerically: neighbouring blocks first touch at **~110–113°** of bend, beyond the 105° joint limit.
- The pivot-to-pivot spacing is now 64 units (was 56), so the arm looks ~15 % longer, matching the real gaps.
- **The AI uses the identical geometry:** `aiComputeAchievableCurve`, `aiSolveAnglesForCurve` and FABRIK use the same constants (`LINK_BOX_CENTER_X`, `LINK_STEP`, `LINK_AXIS_OFFSET`). Fit predictions match the drawing.

### Twitching motors (6 and 7)

**Likely cause.** All motors ran at Position P Gain **1200**. The XL330-M077's factory value is **400**: it has a low gear ratio, so it's fast and very sensitive to gain. When bending other joints changes the load on a motor, a too-stiff controller can break into violent oscillation. Your `local_config.h` already noted that this arm's motors twitch with a high I gain.

**Firmware (`lineform-ai.ino`):** new per-motor stiffness scale **`P_GAIN_PCT_LIST`** (% of the shared P gain, by motor number, 0 or missing = 100). It applies to every stiffness level (`pGainFor()`).
- `local_config.h` sets **motors 6 and 7 to 50 %** (P 600, level 1 → 350). Go lower (e.g. 35) if they still twitch, higher if they sag or feel too soft.
- Documented in `local_config.example.h`.

**GUI safety net (`Motors.pde`, twitch detector):**
- While a motor holds in AI mode, its readings are watched. **4+ direction changes of ≥ 2.5° within 1.5 s** counts as a twitch.
- Response: that motor is softened one stiffness level, and the chat says which motor and how far it swung. If it is already at the softest level, it is switched **OFF**.
- Not checked during animations, while you drag that motor's bar, or for motors that are OFF / at 0 %.
- Simulated: fires on ±6° @ 3.5 Hz (0.6 s) and ±3° @ 1.3 Hz. Does **not** fire on noise up to ±1°, a 60° move with overshoot, gravity sag, or a hand push.
- **Reset** now restores full stiffness on every joint, undoing hinge taps and safety-net softening.

## Later the same day (3): all stiffness settings are per arm

- Motors 6 and 7 are back to full stiffness (P 1200): `P_GAIN_PCT_LIST` is all 100 in this arm's `local_config.h`. The GUI's twitch safety net still steps in if they oscillate.
- **Every stiffness setting can now be set in `local_config.h`** (git-ignored, per arm), so a teammate's arm with a stiffer XL330 model can use its own values:
  - `STIFFNESS_P_GAINS_LIST`: P gain per stiffness level (default `1200, 700, 350, 120`). This was hard-coded in the `.ino` before.
  - `DROOP_I_GAIN`: I gain at full stiffness (default 150; this arm 30).
  - `P_GAIN_PCT_LIST`: per-motor %.
  - The defaults in `lineform-ai.ino` are unchanged, so an arm without these lines behaves as before. All three are documented in `local_config.example.h`.
- The GUI no longer keeps its own copy of the P-gain table. The hinge-tap console message now shows only the level.
- Tested: with no override → 1200/700…; with `STIFFNESS_P_GAINS_LIST 1500, 800, 400, 150` + `P_GAIN_PCT_LIST 100, 80` → motor 1 P 1500, motor 2 P 1200.
